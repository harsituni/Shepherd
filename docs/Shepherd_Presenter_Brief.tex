\section{Why we built this (the problem)}
Static evacuation advice (``leave west'', a PDF map, a one-time SMS) fails when \textbf{fire, wind, and traffic change minute by minute}. People clog the same arteries; some shelters become unsafe; phones don't update; commanders can't see closed roads or cut-offs in one place.

\textbf{Defense \& Security fit (theme language from NAT):} emergency response, public safety, resilient infrastructure, situational awareness. Shepherd is a \textbf{decision-support + public routing} layer for urban evacuation --- not a firefighting prediction product and not certified life-safety software (say that honestly if asked).

\section{Vision \& what we actually shipped}
\subsection Vision
One system that (1) sees the hazard grow with real wind/forecast, (2) knows Edmonton's \textbf{real OpenStreetMap roads}, (3) gives each person a \textbf{time-aware} route to an open shelter, (4) \textbf{re-routes live} when roads close, shelters go unsafe, or traffic jams, (5) shows commanders \textbf{closed roads, crowd size, live phone paths, and who's cut off next}.

\section{Ready-to-paste: Project Summary ($\le$250 words)}
Shepherd is a live evacuation platform for Edmonton built for natHacks 2026 Defense \& Security. When a wildfire or tornado spreads with the wind, static ``leave west'' advice fails: roads jam, shelters become unsafe, and people drive into danger. Shepherd connects three surfaces in one system --- a commander dashboard, a public phone web app, and a FastAPI backend --- so routing and situational awareness stay in sync as conditions change.

We simulate wind-driven wildfire and tornado scenarios on real OpenStreetMap roads. A forecast estimates when fire reaches each place; we close road segments thirty minutes before predicted arrival and score routes as ok, tight, or blocked. The phone shows leave-by times and multi-option routes, then keeps re-routing mid-trip when a path goes unsafe or congested. The dashboard shows hazard growth, closed roads, traffic congestion ribbons, live phone paths, cut-off warnings, and an estimate of how many people and cars need to leave near the hazard pin (zone density $\times$ day/time). Traffic uses a standard BPR delay model with anti-gridlock assignment and projects large crowds (on the order of 100k cars) without freezing the demo.

Judges can join from their own phones via a QR link. Shepherd is a working prototype for emergency decision support and public guidance --- not certified life-safety software --- with a clear path to agency validation, authenticated roles, and calibrated demand data.

\section{Ready-to-paste: About the Project ($\le$500 words)}
\textbf{Inspiration.} Alberta's wildfire seasons and urban evacuation stress made the Defense \& Security theme concrete for us: public safety fails when information is static. We wanted one loop where the hazard, the roads, the traffic, the phones, and the commanders all update together --- on a real city (Edmonton), not a toy grid.

\textbf{How we built it.} Five people owned clear surfaces under a shared API contract: FastAPI backend (in-memory state), wildfire/tornado simulation with wind and forecast, OSM-based routing with fire timing and BPR traffic, a MapLibre command dashboard, and a phone web app. We used React/Vite, MapLibre + OSM tiles, OSMnx/NetworkX, SciPy/NumPy/Shapely, and Cloudflare tunnels so guest phones can join the demo. GeoJSON is always [lon, lat]. No database or auth by design for hackathon speed; state lives in memory and resets cleanly.

\textbf{What we learned.} Integration beats isolated features: a beautiful fire model is useless if phones don't reroute and commanders can't see closed roads. Safety margins must be explicit (close at arrival $-$ 30). Large traffic demos need projection (many cars represented by fewer agents) or the API hangs. UX matters under stress --- leave-by countdown, collapsed panels, simulated drive by default with a real GPS start pin, and honest copy when no route exists.

\textbf{Challenges.} Syncing five workstreams without breaking \texttt{main}; keeping /route fast under crowd assignment; painting continuous traffic corridors on a graph; mid-trip reroutes visible on both phone and dashboard; wind direction conventions (FROM degrees); and staying demo-reliable with tunnels/QR. Flood remains deferred; demand uses transparent zone tables rather than live census --- we say that openly.

\textbf{Next.} Calibrated open-census demand, WebSocket push, agency roles and retention limits, live shelter intake, and partnerships with emergency managers to validate the routing rules before any operational use.

\section{Curveball Q\&A (Pitch \& Demonstration)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt]
\item[Production / life-safety certified?] No --- hackathon prototype. Clear safety margin + real OSM; needs agency validation, auth, persistence before any operational use.
\item[Where do $\sim$100k cars come from?] Transparent model: zone density $\times$ hazard footprint $\times$ day/time occupancy $\rightarrow$ people $\rightarrow$ cars; projected onto fewer simulated agents so demos stay real-time.
\item[Why not Google Maps / Waze?] Consumer maps don't take our fire forecast, timed road closures, or shelter safety as first-class inputs, and don't give commanders the same live state.
\item[Why only some roads coloured?] Intentional. Only actionable layers: fire, closed roads, jammed corridors, active phone routes.
\item[Flood / other hazards?] Flood deferred in the contract. Same architecture (hazard polygons + road deadlines + reroute) extends.
\item[How accurate is the fire model?] Wind-driven demo/forecast engine for timing logic, not a full firefighting product. Routing uses \texttt{arrival\_min} and the $-$30 buffer.
\item[Privacy?] No auth in demo; temporary pins (stale $\sim$30s); priority stores type + last 4 only. Production needs consent and retention limits.
\item[What's novel?] End-to-end loop on real Edmonton OSM: wind hazard $\rightarrow$ timed closures $\rightarrow$ live phone reroute $\rightarrow$ commander cut-offs and demand.
\item[Interdisciplinary / team?] Five builders, five surfaces (API, routing, sim, dashboard, phone), one contract --- software engineering + emergency UX + traffic modelling.
\end{description}
